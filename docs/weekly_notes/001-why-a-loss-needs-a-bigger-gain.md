Welcome, and thanks for confirming. Each week this note explains one idea about portfolio risk or position sizing in a few minutes of reading. It is education, not advice, and every number below is a made-up example.

## The asymmetry

A loss and the gain that undoes it are not the same size, because the gain is measured against a smaller number.

Take a hypothetical $10,000 account. After a 20% loss it holds $8,000. A 20% gain on $8,000 is $1,600, which lands at $9,600, still short. To return to $10,000 the account needs $2,000 on a base of $8,000, which is 25%.

## The pattern

The deeper the loss, the faster the required recovery grows. The formula is 1 / (1 − loss) − 1.

- A 10% loss needs about an 11.1% gain.
- A 20% loss needs a 25% gain.
- A 33% loss needs about a 49% gain.
- A 50% loss needs a 100% gain: $10,000 falls to $5,000 and has to double.

## Why it matters when you size a position

The same arithmetic is why a series of equal ups and downs does not break even. In the same hypothetical account, +10% then −10% gives $10,000 × 1.10 × 0.90 = $9,900, not $10,000. The more a holding swings, the more this drag shows up, which is one reason volatility matters beyond how a chart looks.

None of this says what anyone should hold. It is a reminder to read a drawdown figure as a statement about the climb back, and not only about the fall.
