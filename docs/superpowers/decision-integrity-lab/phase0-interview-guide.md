# Phase 0a — Customer discovery interviews (25×)

**Goal:** falsify the Decision Integrity Lab hypothesis before building anything. Learn
whether a real, painful, recurring pain exists around *decision regret* — and whether people
have a *concrete upcoming decision* they'd let someone help them make honestly.

**This is generative, not confirmatory.** You are not pitching the Lab. You are not describing
a solution. If you catch yourself explaining the product, stop and ask another question.

---

## The kill gate (decide before you start, honour it after)

> **≥ 5 of 25** interviews must expose **BOTH**:
> 1. a **recent, painful, specific** decision the person now regrets (or is unsure about), **and**
> 2. a **real, dated, upcoming** decision they are facing.
>
> **< 5 → STOP.** Either the pain isn't there, or the segment is wrong. Narrow the segment
> (e.g. "solo operators making irreversible >$5k calls") and rerun, or shelve the Lab.

Record each interview against the [scorecard](#scorecard) the same day. Do not soften a
"no evidence" into a "maybe" — the whole point is a gate with teeth.

---

## Recruit criteria (who to talk to)

Target people who make **consequential, hard-to-reverse decisions alone or near-alone**, on a
recurring basis, and who feel the cost when they get one wrong. Examples to source from:

- Solo founders / independent operators making capital-allocation or hiring calls.
- Active self-directed investors sizing real positions (the existing GESH audience is a warm pool).
- Freelancers/consultants choosing between engagements or pricing irreversibly.
- Anyone who has said, unprompted, "I keep second-guessing myself on the big ones."

**Avoid:** people who mostly execute others' decisions, or whose decisions are trivially
reversible — no pain, no product.

Aim for **25 completed** conversations. Expect to reach out to ~60–80 to land 25.

---

## Structure (20–30 min, no pitch)

Open: *"I'm researching how people make and live with hard decisions. There's nothing to buy
and nothing to demo — I just want to understand your experience. Can I record for my notes?"*

### 1. The last painful decision (past — this is the core signal)
- "Tell me about a recent decision you're not sure you got right. Walk me through it."
- "What made it hard? What were you missing at the moment you decided?"
- "How did you decide, in the end? What did you write down, if anything?"
- "Looking back — what do you *wish* you'd captured *before* you acted?"
- "What did getting it wrong (or nearly wrong) actually cost you?"

> Listening for: a **specific, recent, painful** instance — not a hypothetical. Vague
> "yeah decisions are hard" ≠ evidence. A concrete story with a real cost = evidence #1.

### 2. Current behaviour (how they cope today)
- "Do you have any system for this? Notes app, spreadsheet, a friend you call, nothing?"
- "Have you ever gone back to check whether a past decision's reasoning held up? How?"
- "What happens when you're wrong — do you find out? How long after?"

> Listening for: the workaround they already pay for in time/money. If they've hacked
> something together, the pain is real. If they've done nothing, probe whether that's because
> it doesn't hurt enough (a red flag for the Lab).

### 3. The upcoming decision (future — evidence #2)
- "Is there a decision coming up that fits this pattern? When do you have to call it?"
- "What would 'no regret later' look like for that one?"

> Listening for: a **real, dated** upcoming decision. "Nothing coming up" is a soft no.

### 4. Willingness signals (record, don't sell)
- "If someone credible helped you make that upcoming call *and* left you a record you could
  check later — would that be worth anything to you? What?"
- Do **not** name a price, describe the Lab, or promise anything. Note their unprompted number.

Close: *"Who else do you know who wrestles with decisions like this?"* (referral chain).

## Anti-bias rules (from the plan's §A)

- **Never describe the solution.** The moment you pitch, answers become worthless.
- **Past behaviour > stated intent.** A story about a real past decision outweighs any
  "yeah I'd totally use that."
- **A dated upcoming decision > enthusiasm.** Warmth without a real next decision is noise.
- **Write the "no" down.** The gate only works if you record disconfirming evidence honestly.
