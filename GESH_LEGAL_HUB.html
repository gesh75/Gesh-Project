3 · The core question — publisher's exclusion vs. investment adviser

The one question everything hinges on
Is GESH a "publisher" (excluded from the Investment Advisers Act — no registration) or an "investment adviser" (regulated)? The research says GESH sits right on the line. Only a lawyer can rule on it — but the answer decides the whole path.

The test — Lowe v. SEC (1985). A publication is excluded if it is:

  • Impersonal — generally available to all subscribers, not individualized to one person's situation;
  • Bona fide — accurate genuine commentary, not a front for touting securities the publisher holds;
  • Of general & regular circulation — a real publication, not advice issued "in response to episodic market activity" for a specific client.
Filtering / alerts / letting users pick which general content to see does not break the exclusion (the 2024 Seeking Alpha analysis).

✅ Where GESH is SAFE (publisher side)

  • The masked $100K "model book" view of the owner's portfolio — impersonal, identical for everyone.
  • Generic per-ticker research (analyze-ticker, momentum, news, AI research card) — general circulation.
  • The public /track-record proof page — provided numbers are honest & labeled hypothetical/simulated (now true after the phantom-POL fix).

The three possible answers from counsel
(A) Fine as-is with stronger disclaimers & "educational, not personalized advice" framing. Cheapest.
(B) Fine only with framing/UX changes (never "you should"; present as impersonal "how the model scores this"; educational-use gate). Moderate — mostly copy/UX, Claude implements.
(C) Registration required → likely Michigan state RIA (not SEC; SEC is $100M+ AUM), Series 65 + Form ADV + compliance program. Most expensive; usually avoidable for a solo educational tool by accepting (B).
Most solo research/education platforms live in (A)/(B). The "My Book on your own holdings" feature is what makes (B) more likely than (A).

7 · AI marketing claims — don't get "AI-washed" DIY

In March 2024 the SEC fined Delphia ($225k) and Global Predictions ($175k) for AI claims they couldn't substantiate. Rule: you must be able to prove every AI claim. GESH genuinely uses Claude/Gemini/Ollama — substantiable — so this is manageable.

  • Do: describe AI accurately ("AI-generated research using Anthropic Claude"); keep the "AI opinion, not a calculation" labels already added to the risk score / fair value.
  • Don't: "AI that beats the market", "proven AI predictions", or imply a documented edge you can't prove. Keep the paper track record labeled hypothetical / simulated everywhere (also aligns with the Marketing Rule's hostility to hypothetical performance in general-circulation ads).

8 · Engaging counsel — cost + how

What kind of lawyer: a US investment-adviser / securities regulatory attorney (not a generic startup lawyer, not a litigator). Search for "RIA formation", "fintech regulatory", "investment adviser registration", "regulatory opinion". Boutique RIA/fintech firms are cheaper than large firms (GT/WSGR).

Efficient path for a solo founder:

  • Ask for a fixed-fee "regulatory classification memo" answering §3 — the single highest-value deliverable, often quotable flat.
  • Separately, a fixed-fee ToS + Privacy review/redline.
  • Consider a paid 1-hour consult first: bring the attorney brief, get a read on A/B/C, then decide how much further legal work is needed.

Rough ranges (get real quotes): classification memo ≈ low-thousands–$10k · ToS + privacy review low-thousands · full RIA registration multi-thousand + ongoing compliance (only if outcome C). A compliance consultant / "RIA-in-a-box" can be cheaper than a law firm for registration mechanics if you must register.

👉 Hand the attorney GESH_ATTORNEY_ENGAGEMENT_BRIEF.md — it's written to get fixed-fee quotes and keep billable time on judgment, not fact-gathering.
